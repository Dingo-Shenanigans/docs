Studio builds a level from a scene of yours, with the rest of the mod, each time the mod is built. It writes the map's [`reskate-levels.json`](/mods/maps) for you, gives the map its own loading screen and pause map, and installs it to test. For how the game keeps a level, see [maps](/assets/maps).

<Note>
A mod holds one map. Give a second map a mod of its own.
</Note>

## What Studio builds a map from

| Scene | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| A Blender scene (`.blend`) | Everything the [Blender add-on](/studio/blender-addon) sets: collision, surfaces, grinds, lights, the spawn, sounds, effects, routes, materials. The most complete way. |
| An `.fbx` | Meshes, materials and lamps. Surfaces, grinds and the spawn come only from Studio's map editor. |
| A `.glb` or `.gltf` | The same as an `.fbx`. |
| A map folder (`map.json` and what it names) | What the tool that wrote it set, such as the add-on's **Write the Map Folder**. Drop its `map.json` on the window. It cannot be changed in Studio. |

Studio reads `.blend` and `.fbx` files through Blender, without its window. Set `blender.exe` in **Settings**, under **Blender, for maps and for editing a model**, and press **Install or update Studio's Blender add-on** there. A `.glb` needs no Blender.

The map editor also opens **Skater XL and Unity map bundles**. A map from one has not been built and checked in the game yet.

## Adding a map

Pick **Map** on the start page and choose the scene, or drop a `.blend`, `.fbx` or `.glb` on the window and choose **This mod's map**. The map's page opens: the scene in the middle, where you fly around it, and the map's parts down the right.

## Scene

- **Name**: the name players pick the map by.
- **Map ID**: the level's name in the game's files, and its place in the map list. Lowercase letters, digits and single underscores, at most 30. Keep it the same to update a map that is installed.
- **The scene file**, with **Choose another...** and **Show it in its folder**.
- **Blender**: where Studio found it, or **Not found. Choose blender.exe in Settings.**

## Objects

**Objects** lists what is in the scene. Pick something, in the list or in the view, to see it under **What is picked**: to light it, or give it a surface, a grind or a sound. Studio keeps these edits beside the scene, and every build applies them; the scene file itself is not changed.

For an object of the scene you can:

- leave it out of the map, or make it collision only (it collides but is not drawn);
- choose its collision: **Triangle mesh**, **Convex parts**, **Convex hull**, **None (render only)** or **Water**;
- give it a surface (**Search surfaces...**), with its sound, grip and behaviour;
- make it a round rail, ground smooth like the game's tubes;
- leave it off the pause map.

A material picked here can be made collision only, given a surface, and given a transparency and a kind (surface, decal, foliage, ocean).

The scene's own objects cannot be moved in Studio: move them in Blender. What you add in the editor can be moved, turned and scaled.

**Add to the map** adds:

| Thing | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Player spawn** | Where the skater starts. **Move the spawn** puts it elsewhere. |
| **Bus stop** | A fast-travel point, with its name and shelter. |
| **Point light**, **Spot light**, **Area light** | A lamp, with its colour, power, range, cone and the times of day it shines. |
| **Audio volume** | A box of one of the game's ambience sounds. |
| **Grind curve** | An invisible rail you draw point by point, with its radius and surface. |
| **NPC route** | A path pedestrians or cars follow. |

**Place a game mesh...** copies one of the game's meshes into the map, and **Place a .glb...** places a model of yours. Both become the map's own geometry, without the game's material, collision or detail levels. A placed game mesh has not been checked in the game yet.

### What only Blender gives

Reflection volumes, sound emitters and speakers, the game's effects, and props of the mod are set in Blender, not in Studio's map editor. See [the Blender add-on](/studio/blender-addon) and [placing a prop in the mod's map](/studio/props#placing-a-prop-in-the-mods-map).

## The spawn

A map has one start point. With more than one spawn marker, the add-on warns and uses the first. The [`reskate-levels.json`](/mods/maps) Studio writes names it. Without a spawn, the build falls back on San Vansterdam's start point, which is not on your map: give every map one.

## Surfaces and grinds

A surface is one of the game's: it sets the sound, the sparks, and how the board grips, slides and behaves. Set it on a material, or on an object to cover the whole of it.

The game works out what can be ground from the collision: hard edges of the right shape grind by themselves. For anything else, draw a grind curve: an invisible rail along a coping, a pipe or a ledge. Mark a round tube as a round rail so it grinds smoothly.

## Light

**Time of day** is **Morning**, **Noon**, **Afternoon**, **Evening** or **Night**. The map is lit by the game's own sky at that hour, with its sun, fog and exposure. Lamps placed in the scene, or added under **Objects**, light it as well, each at the times of day it is set to shine.

A sun in a Blender scene is left out. Not yet: a sky of the map's own, and baked or bounced light. A sun of the map's own is only an option of `compile-map`, which builds a map alone, outside a mod.

## Materials

A map's materials come from its scene: their pictures, transparency and kind, set in Blender's **Material** panel. Studio maps each onto one of the game's world shaders. The map editor has no **Materials** part; another game shader or shader code for a map's material is set on the command line. See [materials](/studio/materials#a-maps-materials).

## Loading

- **Streaming**: **Automatic: big maps load in pieces**, **Off: the whole map loads at once**, or **On**. Off is right for a small map; a big one takes long to load and may not fit in memory.
- **Piece size**: **200 m**, **100 m** or **50 m, the least hitching**.
- **Load distance**: how far ahead pieces load, 60 to 1,000 m. Raise it if parts of the map appear late.
- **Make simpler copies of meshes for far away**: detail levels for the map's meshes. Their distances are not settable yet.

## Pictures

Studio makes the map's pictures from the scene, unless you give your own.

- **Pause map**: **Flat** or **3D with shadows**, for the picture made from the scene.
- **Your own pictures**: put any of these, as `.png`, `.jpg` or `.dds`, in `assets\<scene name>\` beside the scene. **Open that folder** opens it.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `map` | The pause map |
| `load_screen` | The loading screen |
| `map_logo` | The map's logo |
| `map_clouds` | Clouds over the pause map |

Each says **yours** once the file is there, or **made for you**. The picture is read at every build.

## Building and testing

**Test in game**, in the top bar, builds the whole mod with the map and starts ReSkate. See [testing](/studio/testing).

The map's **Build** part has **Build and install**: it builds the mod with this map and puts it in skate.'s `Mods` folder, without starting the game. Close skate. first. It needs a scene, a name and a map ID, and a saved mod. While it builds, it says what it is doing, with **Stop**. After it:

- **What was built**: **Triangles**, **Collision pieces**, **Lights**, and for a streamed map **Pieces it loads in** and **Meshes with simpler copies**;
- **What the build said**: the build's log, with **Copy it**.

The map appears in the game's map lists under **CUSTOM MAPS**: see [maps](/mods/maps#where-custom-maps-appear). To share it, see [publishing](/studio/publishing).

## On the command line

```powershell
reskate_cli import "C:\Mods\My Map" "D:\Skate" map "C:\Scenes\park.blend" --name "My Park" --id my_park
reskate_cli map show "C:\Mods\My Map"
reskate_cli map set "C:\Mods\My Map" time-of-day evening
reskate_cli build "C:\Mods\My Map" "D:\Skate" "C:\Work" --install
```

`map show` lists every setting with what it takes: `time-of-day`, `streaming`, `cell-size`, `cell-load-distance`, `mesh-lods`, `include-mods` and `pause-map`. Edits made under **Objects** are the window's only. See the [command line](/studio/command-line).
