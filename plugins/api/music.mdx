---
title: "Music"
description: "reskate.music: read and control the music an outside player plays and the game's own soundtrack, and play the music through a speaker you place with reskate.spatialize_music."
---

`reskate.music` reads the song playing and skips it, and [`reskate.spatialize_music`](#a-speaker-in-the-world) plays the music through a speaker you place. Every function, and the `track_changed` event, needs the `music` permission; calling one without it raises an error. See [permissions](/plugins/permissions).

There are two kinds of music:

- **An outside player**: Spotify or another app playing on the player's computer, read and controlled through Windows' own media controls (the ones the media keys use). `current`, `play`, `pause`, `next` and `previous` work on it.
- **The game's own soundtrack**, on the player's headphones. `game` and `game_next` work on it. While a Spotify station is on, the outside player's song is the one heard, and the game's soundtrack has no song.

`play`, `pause`, `next`, `previous` and `game_next` share a budget of 2 calls a second.

## An outside player

### `reskate.music.current()`

The song the outside player has now. The first call starts ReSkate listening to it.

<ResponseField name="track" type="table or nil">
`nil` when there is no outside player to read.
<Expandable title="fields">
<ResponseField name="app" type="string">The app playing it, by its application id as Windows names it.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="title" type="string">The song's title.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="artist" type="string">Its artist.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="album" type="string">Its album.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="playing" type="boolean">False while it is paused.</ResponseField>
</Expandable>
</ResponseField>

```lua
local track = reskate.music.current()
if track and track.playing then
    reskate.ui.toast(track.artist .. " - " .. track.title)
end
```

<Note>Needs the `music` permission.</Note>

### `reskate.music.play()`

Tells the outside player to play.

<ResponseField name="ok" type="boolean">True when the command was sent. It does not say an app took it.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="why" type="string">`"more than 2 a second"` when `ok` is false.</ResponseField>

<Note>Needs the `music` permission.</Note>

### `reskate.music.pause()`

Tells the outside player to pause.

<ResponseField name="ok" type="boolean">True when the command was sent.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="why" type="string">`"more than 2 a second"` when `ok` is false.</ResponseField>

<Note>Needs the `music` permission.</Note>

### `reskate.music.next()`

Tells the outside player to skip to the next song.

<ResponseField name="ok" type="boolean">True when the command was sent.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="why" type="string">`"more than 2 a second"` when `ok` is false.</ResponseField>

```lua
reskate.input.bind("skip", "Pad.Back+Pad.RB")
reskate.on("action", function(name)
    if name == "skip" then reskate.music.next() end
end)
```

<Note>Needs the `music` permission (and `input` for the example's bind).</Note>

### `reskate.music.previous()`

Tells the outside player to go back a song.

<ResponseField name="ok" type="boolean">True when the command was sent.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="why" type="string">`"more than 2 a second"` when `ok` is false.</ResponseField>

<Note>Needs the `music` permission.</Note>

## The game's soundtrack

### `reskate.music.game()`

The song the game's own soundtrack plays now.

<ResponseField name="track" type="table or nil">
`nil` while a Spotify station is on, or before a song has started.
<Expandable title="fields">
<ResponseField name="app" type="string">Always `"skate."`.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="title" type="string">The song's title.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="artist" type="string">Its artist.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="album" type="string">Always empty.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="playing" type="boolean">Always true.</ResponseField>
</Expandable>
</ResponseField>

```lua
local song = reskate.music.game()
if song then reskate.log("now playing " .. song.title .. " by " .. song.artist) end
```

<Note>Needs the `music` permission.</Note>

### `reskate.music.game_next()`

Skips the game's soundtrack to its next song.

<ResponseField name="ok" type="boolean">True when the song was skipped.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="why" type="string">Why not: `"asked more than twice in a second"` or `"the game's music cannot skip now (a Spotify station is on, or no song has started)"`.</ResponseField>

<Note>Needs the `music` permission. Shares the budget of 2 calls a second with the outside player's commands.</Note>

## A speaker in the world

### `reskate.spatialize_music(speaker)`

Plays the music through a speaker your plugin places: a boombox on the ground, a car stereo, a club's sound system. Your plugin works out where the speaker is relative to the listener and passes the result. ReSkate applies it to the music as the game streams it:

- the song a Spotify station is playing, and
- any other stream of 45 seconds or more: the game's own songs, and songs from the world's speakers.

Shorter streams are the world's short music loops. `mute_loops` silences them instead. Each long stream has its own filters, so the two songs of a crossfade never share a filter's state.

<ParamField path="speaker" type="table, nil or false" required>
The speaker's settings, or `nil` or `false` to turn it off at once.
<Expandable title="fields">
<ParamField path="active" type="boolean" default="true">`false` turns it off, as passing `nil` does.</ParamField>
<ParamField path="left" type="number" default="1">The left ear's gain, 0 to 4.</ParamField>
<ParamField path="right" type="number" default="1">The right ear's gain, 0 to 4.</ParamField>
<ParamField path="lpf" type="number" default="1">Both ears' low-pass, 0.04 to 1. 1 lets everything through; lower is more muffled, as through a wall.</ParamField>
<ParamField path="lpf_l" type="number" default="lpf">The left ear's low-pass, 0.04 to 1, in place of `lpf`.</ParamField>
<ParamField path="lpf_r" type="number" default="lpf">The right ear's low-pass, 0.04 to 1, in place of `lpf`.</ParamField>
<ParamField path="itd" type="number" default="0">The delay between the ears, in samples, -32 to 32. Negative delays the left ear. Taken as a whole number.</ParamField>
<ParamField path="env" type="number" default="0">How much of a small room's reverb is mixed in, 0 to 0.65.</ParamField>
<ParamField path="mute_loops" type="boolean" default="true">Silences the world's short music loops while the station's song plays through the speaker. Only then: with no station song going through it, the loops play.</ParamField>
</Expandable>
</ParamField>

<ResponseField name="on" type="boolean">True when the speaker is on now. Always `false` on a dedicated server, which plays no music.</ResponseField>

A number past its range is held to it. A number that is not finite raises an error, and so does anything but a table, `nil` or `false`.

A setting lasts about 2 seconds. Call it every tick you want the speaker. When your plugin stops or crashes, the music is back as it was within 2 seconds. Turned on again after it was off or ran out, the speaker starts with fresh filters.

```lua
local speaker = vector.create(12, 0, -40)

reskate.on("tick", function()
    local me = reskate.skater.position()
    if not me then return end
    local distance = vector.magnitude(speaker - me)
    local gain = math.clamp(8 / math.max(distance, 1), 0, 1)
    reskate.spatialize_music({
        left = gain, right = gain,
        lpf = math.clamp(1 - distance / 60, 0.1, 1),
        env = 0.2,
    })
end)
```

A real speaker pans: work out which side of the listener it is on (with the camera's `right` from [`reskate.camera.get`](/plugins/api/camera), which needs `camera`) and give that ear more gain, a clearer low-pass and the earlier sound (`itd`). The example above needs `skater.read` for the skater's position.

<Note>Needs the `music` permission. It changes only how the music sounds on your game. Plugins cannot change the game's volume or sound settings.</Note>

## The `track_changed` event

Fired when anything about the outside player's song changes: another song, or it pauses or plays. ReSkate looks once a second. The game's own soundtrack does not fire it.

```lua
reskate.on("track_changed", function(track)
    if track.playing then
        reskate.ui.toast("Now playing: " .. track.title)
    end
end)
```

<ResponseField name="app" type="string">The app playing it, by its application id as Windows names it.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="title" type="string">The song's title.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="artist" type="string">Its artist.</ResponseField>
<ResponseField name="playing" type="boolean">False while it is paused.</ResponseField>

The event's table has no `album`; call `reskate.music.current()` for it. Registering a handler needs the `music` permission. See [events](/plugins/events).
